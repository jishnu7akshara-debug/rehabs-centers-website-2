How we treat

Most plans combine two or three of these. Your physiotherapist picks them after the assessment, not before.

Manual therapy

Joint mobilisation, soft-tissue release and stretching to calm pain and get stiff joints moving. It's the hands-on part of most first sessions.

  • Stiff shoulders and necks
  • Back pain flare-ups
  • Post-surgery stiffness

Exercise therapy

Progressive strength and control work with bands, weights and bodyweight. This is what keeps pain from coming back, so you'll leave every session with a short home plan.

  • Weak or unstable joints
  • Return to sport
  • Desk-related pain

Kinesiology taping

Elastic tape that supports a joint or muscle while you keep training. Useful between sessions and on match days.

  • Knee and shoulder support
  • Lower back offloading
  • Swelling after sprains

Electrotherapy

TENS, interferential and ultrasound therapy to settle pain early on, so you can start moving sooner. Always alongside exercise, never instead of it.

  • Acute pain and spasm
  • Nerve pain
  • Muscle re-activation

Clinical Pilates

Reformer sessions led by a physiotherapist, built around your injury. Springs take load off the joint so you can strengthen it earlier.

  • Chronic back pain
  • Pregnancy and postnatal
  • Hypermobility

Balance and gait training

Wobble boards, step work and walking practice for anyone who feels unsteady, after an ankle injury, a stroke or simply with age.

  • Fear of falling
  • Repeated ankle sprains
  • After stroke

What recovery looks like

A typical plan for a sports injury or a bad back. Yours will be set after the first assessment.

  1. Week 1

    Assess and settle the pain

    A full movement assessment, a clear explanation of the problem, and treatment to calm things down.

  2. Weeks 2 to 3

    Restore movement

    Hands-on work drops off as you take over with exercises. Most people feel the change here.

  3. Weeks 4 to 6

    Build strength

    Heavier, more specific exercise for your sport, job or daily life. Sessions become less frequent.

  4. Week 8 onwards

    Back to normal, and staying there

    A re-test against your first assessment and a maintenance plan you can follow on your own.

Questions people ask

Do I need a doctor's referral?

No. You can book directly. If your assessment shows something that needs a scan or a specialist, we'll tell you and refer you.

How many sessions will I need?

Most people need between 4 and 8. We'll give you an estimate at the end of your first session and update it as you improve.

What should I wear?

Loose clothes you can move in. Shorts for knee and ankle problems, a vest or loose top for shoulders and neck.

Does insurance cover physiotherapy?

Many policies cover physiotherapy after surgery or hospital admission. We provide itemised invoices for reimbursement.

Can I cancel or reschedule?

Yes, free of charge up to 4 hours before your session. Just message us on WhatsApp.
